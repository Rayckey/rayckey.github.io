01 / ABOUT ME

Rocky Wang

I’m Weiqi (Rocky) Wang, a robotics engineer working on robot learning, manipulation, locomotion, and motion planning. I completed my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering at UCLA in September 2026, conducting my doctoral research in the Center for Vision, Cognition, Learning, and Autonomy (VCLA).

My research focuses on making robots more useful in environments shared with people. My work has improved robots’ access to spaces and objects, reduced reliance on human demonstrations for learning manipulation skills, and demonstrated the transfer of skills from simulation to physical robots. These results support the broader goal of making robots easier to teach and deploy while accounting for their physical capabilities and how people use their surroundings.

Before my Ph.D., I was an Algorithm Engineer at CloudMinds/INNFOS, developing control software for the XR1 mobile humanoid and locomotion control for quadrupeds. I worked on wearable-IMU teleoperation, robot dynamics identification, impedance and admittance control, and walking and trotting gait generation. During internships at OffWorld, I developed dual-arm motion planning, force-feedback control, and simulation for autonomous mining robots.

I received my M.S. in Mechanical Engineering from Johns Hopkins University in 2017 and my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering, with a minor in Mathematics, from the University of Miami in 2015. At Johns Hopkins’ Laboratory for Computational Sensing + Robotics, I worked on surgical robotics, including haptic training simulations, real-time 3D reconstruction for shape sensing, and virtual fixtures for collaborative ultrasound imaging.
